**Prerequisites:** Junior standing, and at least one philosophy course other
than 240, or permission of instructor.

**Course Description:** The readings and discussion for this course will focus
on issues surrounding meaning, truth, and reference in language, and the way
they arise in the various uses to which language is put. We will be primarily
reading historically influential works of philosophy and linguistics from the
20th century. We will start with the thought that the meaning of a word is the
thing it stands for, but we will quickly see several of the ways in which it
must be more complex. We will also discuss the extent to which linguistic
meaning is constituted by the intentions of the speaker, the notions of truth
or falsity, and the social, historical, and biological context of the speaker.

**Learning Outcomes:** As in many other philosophy classes, students will
further develop skills of summarizing the arguments of others, putting forward
their own arguments both in writing and in conversation, and analyzing the ways
in which these arguments do and don't succeed. Specific to the topic, students
will gain a familiarity with several historically influential views in
philosophy and linguistics about the nature of meaning and the way it is
constituted in language.

## Paper Topics

**Paper 1:** Choose some communicative device or speech act, and explain how it
works. That is, give a definition of what it is, an explanation of what it
means, and a description of conditions under which it is correct or incorrect
to use it, or when it succeeds or fails at doing what it is intended to do.
Make sure to consider some borderline cases to show that your conditions are
necessary and sufficient.

Some possible ideas for a communicative device or speech act: turn signals in a
car, giving a name to a child,
[First World Problems Woman](http://knowyourmeme.com/memes/first-world-problems),
saying "hello", etc. Some sorts of borderline cases you might consider:
intentionally misusing it (is there a way to successfully misuse it or are they
always failures?), accidentally using it, using it where no one else can see it
(either with or without your knowledge that no one is around), etc.

The paper should be about 2000 words long (approx. 4-7 pages), though it may be
somewhat longer or a bit shorter if that is what is needed for the analysis.
Due by **11:59 pm on Friday, November 6.** Please send me an e-mail by **11:59
on Monday, October 19** telling me the communicative device or speech act you
have chosen, and a few quick bullet points outlining what you think the use
conditions are and/or some attempted use cases that you might use in the paper
to argue your point. If you want to send me an outline or a draft some time
along the way, I'd also be willing to give you some feedback on that.

**Paper 2:** Choose one of the following topics, or suggest another one to me.
The paper should be about 2000 words long (approx. 4-7 pages), though it may be
somewhat longer or a bit shorter if that is what is needed for the analysis.
When you start thinking about the paper, you should send me an idea of the
topic so that I can give you a sense of whether I think it is likely to work
out effectively. If you want to send me an outline or a draft some time along
the way, I'd also be willing to give you some feedback on that.

- Millikan, Grice, and Putnam give three very different ways that the meaning of language depends on social interaction. Explain two of them and compare and contrast.
- Rudolf Carnap (in "Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology") advocates the creation of idealized languages for scientific purposes. Kripke, Putnam, Millikan, and others say that much of the meaning of language depends on its social history. Explain one of these ideas and show either how it would cause problems for Carnap's approach or how he could get around it.
- For the empiricists, meaning is related to knowledge (i.e., how we can verify a sentence), while for Grice and Austin, meaning is related to action (i.e., what we do with a sentence). Show how this contrast can help shed some light on ideas illustrated by one of the other authors we read.
- Langton emphasizes the ways that certain speech acts can't function when a potential hearer makes certain assumptions about the speaker. In light of Millikan's ideas, how would this affect the development of the language over time?
