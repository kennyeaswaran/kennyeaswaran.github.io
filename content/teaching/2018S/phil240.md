**Course Description:**

This course introduces students to the central concepts of deductive and
inductive logic. This gives students training and practice in abstract
reasoning of a type applicable to every subject matter. The specific method
involves the construction and analysis of very precise artificial languages to
supplement the imprecise natural languages we speak.

We begin with the general conceptual issues of deductive logic, as the study of
what conclusion *must* be true if some premises are true. We develop this in
detail starting first with the role of Boolean logic (and, or, not), and
continuing with conditionals and quantifiers (if/then, every, some). We also
discuss the concepts of probability and inductive logic - what it takes for
some premises to give us a *good reason to believe* a conclusion, even if they
don't guarantee it.
