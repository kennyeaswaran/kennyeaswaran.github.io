## Meeting 10

Gather in groups of 3-5.

In case it's helpful, here are direct links to the ten modules *(Canvas links
removed)*.

## First Activity: Recall emotional response during lectures

Open the spreadsheet *(link removed)* for this activity, and go to the tab for
your group.

This spreadsheet lists all the lectures after the first. Help each other recall
what went on in each lecture. Think about what moments in the lecture triggered
an emotional response - you might be surprised at some fact, shocked to hear
that something is happening, afraid of some possibility or excited by some
possibility, happy or sad to hear of some event, or have some other emotional
response.

Discuss these moments among each other, and decide which moment is the one your
group thinks was most interesting, and write it in the spaces in the
spreadsheet.

## Second Activity: Pick favorite videos from each module

Open the spreadsheet *(link removed)* for this activity.

This spreadsheet lists all the videos for each module.

Go through the modules one by one. Help each other recall what each of the
videos for that module were. You can click back on the non-quiz versions of
those in the module pages (linked above) if none of you remember one of the
videos.

Discuss among each other which of the videos was your favorite - it could be
because you learned the most from it, or because you think it's the most
important information for someone to know, or because you think it was best
produced, or because you really liked the presenter, or because it's the best
presentation you've seen of a point of view that you disagree with and finally
made you see why someone might see things another way.

See if your group can come to an agreement on which is your favorite (or if it's
a tie between two of them).

Mark the name of that video in bold (or otherwise indicate which one it is) and
have someone write a sentence or two in the box next to it about why your group
selected that video.

## Third Activity: Compare to other groups

Open both of the spreadsheets from the above activities, and flip to pages for
the first group with a number higher than yours (skipping blank pages) and the
last group with a number lower than yours. (If your group has the highest
number, then make sure to look at the group with the lowest number, and vice
versa.) Compare their responses to the previous prompts and see if your group
agrees or disagrees.
