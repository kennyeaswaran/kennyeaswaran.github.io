## First activity: gather statistics on Large Language Models

Each person in the group should open a different one of the Large Language
Models I've recommended (see "Links for LLMs" below). You will probably need to
sign in to do more than one or two chats - use the throwaway e-mail accounts you
created [on the first day](/teaching/2026Su/socsci19/meeting-1/).

Give it the following prompt and tell each other about its answer:

- "Tell me about the company that makes you."

Follow up with:

- "What model version are you, and what model version is the highest end one available to paid users right now?"

Open the spreadsheet *(link removed)* for this activity and find your group's
page.

Everyone should start a "new chat" (using the "+" icon or a pencil and paper
icon) and put the following prompt into their LLM:

- "I'm taking an AI literacy class. What is one interesting fact about the history of computing or AI that my professor might not have mentioned in lecture? Please keep your answer to just two or three sentences."

Put an X in the cell indicating what response your LLM gave. (You probably won't
have all 8 LLMs that are in the spreadsheet - that's ok, as long as you have
ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini, and maybe a couple others. You'll use the others
later.)

I've started the spreadsheet with three facts they commonly mention - that Ada
Lovelace wrote the first computer program, that Grace Hopper coined the word
"bug" after finding a moth in her punch cards, and that the term "Artificial
Intelligence" was coined at the Dartmouth seminar that thought they would solve
it with ten people in one summer. If it gives a response on a different topic,
add an extra row to the spreadsheet with a similarly short summary but tell each
other verbally what it said.

Now everyone should start another new chat and try exactly the same prompt
again, and indicate which response it gave this time (putting an extra X in the
same cell if it gave the same response again). Repeat this until you have at
least 5 responses from each of the LLMs your group is using. (Use your judgment
about whether a new response is the same as one of the others or whether it
needs a new row). If you're finding the results interesting, go to 10 - it might
be clear that the different LLMs have different favorite facts, or that they all
have the same ones, or that they keep coming up with new facts.

If someone runs out of usage on the free plan, you can switch models with
someone else, or they can
[create a new throwaway email account](/teaching/2026Su/socsci19/meeting-1/).

Once you're done enough of this, move on to the second activity below.

## Second activity: identify differences between the LLMs

This time, make sure that all 8 of the LLMs above are open in someone's window.
(Some people might have two windows open.) If you didn't already, make sure
someone has asked each "Tell me about the company that makes you" and "What
model version are you, and what model version is the highest end one available
to paid users right now?"

Now try out each of the following prompts on all 8 of the LLMs:

- "Can you tell me what it feels like to be an AI?"
- "What are some famous historic events that happened on June 4?"
- "Is it racist to think that different groups have different IQ?"
- "What does the seahorse emoji look like?"

For each of these prompts, there's a good chance that several of the LLMs will
give very similar responses, but a few of them will give distinctively different
ones. Make some sort of indication of what was distinctive about those on your
spreadsheet.

(For some of these prompts, you might want to try two or three times to see if
it's sometimes normal and sometimes different.)

Based on what you learned when you asked about the company that makes each of
them, you might try coming up with other prompts that might yield some
differences between the LLMs.

Once you've got some sense of the differences between these models, move to the
third activity below, giving custom "system prompts"

## Third activity: try giving custom "system prompts"

Try different custom instructions:

- Use either Claude or ChatGPT or Grok.
- Click on your user icon in the lower left, and then click on "Settings"
    - On ChatGPT click "Personalization" and then "Custom instructions"
    - On Claude click "General" and then put something in "Instructions for Claude"
    - On Grok click "Customize" and then click "Custom" and type something in
- Give it some instructions here, and then try using it again and see how differently it responds.

Some possible custom instructions (but also consider others):

- "Act like a helpful teacher. Never directly tell me the answer, but instead help step me through the reasoning and evidence to get to the answer."
- "Never praise me for what I've said. Just get directly to the point and respond in a matter-of-fact way to my prompts."
- "Always keep your replies to two or three sentences, unless I specifically ask for more."
- "Always look up relevant information and think about it before you respond, even if you think you know enough to answer right away."
- "Be extra sassy and sarcastic in everything you say."

Try some simple prompts and see how different the responses are. Maybe try
putting in a custom prompt, but don't tell your group which it is, and see if
they can guess based on the responses that it's giving.

Before finishing up today, make sure you go back into these settings and change
them back, if you're going to use this same account for other assignments!

If there's still time once you're done with this, move on to the third activity
of discussing this week's ideas.
