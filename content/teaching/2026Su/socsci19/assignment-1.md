Think about a hobby or interest that you have, that you have experience with,
and that you have strong opinions on. It might be strategies in some video
game, types of dance moves in a particular style you do, regional cuisine from
your grandparents' village, which intersections in your hometown have the worst
traffic, which classrooms at UCI are the best, whatever. Don't pick a factual
topic that you could learn about through reading books and papers - pick
something that involves participating, or attending, or spectating, or being
part of a community.

Start a new chat with ChatGPT (use a free account, not a paid one, see below).
Come up with some question or argument on this topic and have ChatGPT summarize
the arguments for or against. Carefully read its answer and see if there are
any parts where you think it's making mistakes. Think about which of the
following types of mistakes it's making:

- **Hallucination** - just making up things that are incorrect, that aren't common beliefs
- **Missing things** - not including information that would be important to mention
- **Perspective** - giving one biased perspective rather than a general one, or conversely being too even-handed about perspectives that are incorrect
- **Common misconceptions** - repeating ideas that many people believe, though they are definitely incorrect
- **Sycophancy** - saying that you are totally right and your questions and comments are the best
- Others?

Don't tell it about the mistakes, just keep asking follow-up questions, and see
how far it digs itself into a hole, or if it fixes things. See how many
different mistakes you can make it say. See if you can understand what sorts of
things it is getting right or wrong - think about what you would say if a
student turned this in for a class you were teaching.

At the top of the chat, there should be a button marked "Share" - click that,
and "copy link", and paste this link into a file. Also in this file, write a
couple sentences explaining what kinds of mistakes ChatGPT is making. Submit
that file.
