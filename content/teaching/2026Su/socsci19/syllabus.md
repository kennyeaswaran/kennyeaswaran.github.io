## Course Description

Introduction to the basics of contemporary artificial intelligence. Distinction
between symbolic and neural AIs. Generative AI, prediction engines,
recommendation systems, machine perception. Near-term ethical issues,
medium-term economic implications, long-term risk and safety.

## Student Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will know the differences between different
types of AI systems, be able to analyze the ways that different AI systems
might be applied or developed, and develop skills of using AI effectively and
critically.

## Required Materials and Technology

All readings and lectures will be posted on Canvas.

Students will need to have access to a computer with online meeting
capabilities (fast internet connection, video, and audio) during the required
live interaction times. Headphones are recommended, but not required.

Students will need to be able to watch videos and interact with various online
AI resources for asynchronous activities.

## Assignment Details

There are three types of required weekly assignments:

- One set of asynchronous activities is to watch the video lectures, and do other assigned video viewings and readings - videos will have quizzes embedded to ensure you are watching.
- A second set of asynchronous activities will be turned in for grading. Sometimes this will involve writing up answers to some questions, sometimes this will involve producing something in collaboration with an artificial intelligence, and some other formats might occur.
- The third set of required activities is participation in the weekly online meetings, where we will do interactive activities to work with AI.

One of the early assignments includes watching or reading an “assigned
fiction.” You will choose (or be randomly assigned) early on from a list of
about 20 fictions that each depict some type of artificial intelligence, and in
later weeks, many of the assignments will involve explaining how that fiction
does or doesn’t exemplify some of the topics of that week’s lectures/readings.
You will also be required to give a short final presentation on this fiction.
Several of the weekly assignments will involve putting together parts of this
presentation.

## AI Policy

- Each assignment will describe what use of AI you should or shouldn't make

## Grading Methods

| | |
|---|---:|
| Quizzes on videos | 30% |
| Asynchronous assignments | 30% |
| Attendance at online meetings | 30% |
| Final project | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Attendance at weekly meetings will be graded based on participation.

Video quizzes (including lectures) are graded on correctness - but you can
re-take them multiple times before the deadline.

Other asynchronous activities (including final presentation) will be graded 50%
on completion, 20% for providing feedback on others, 30% based on quality of
work.
