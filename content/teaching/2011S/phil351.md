U ^ (S v C)

Final Exercise 3 - create a proof to show the argument is valid.

Some comments about the argument:

Before you go about trying to create the proof, you should think about why the
argument is valid. To do this, note what each statement says.

1. For every chicken, there was an egg that came before it.
2. For every egg, there was a chicken that came before it.
3. If x came before y and y came before z, then x came before z.
4. Nothing came before itself.
5. There has been at least one chicken.

Conclusion. There have been at least two chickens.

An interesting observation, which is irrelevant to actually constructing the
proof: the reasoning that makes this argument work could be extended to show
that there have been at least three chickens, at least four chickens, and so
on - thus, the premises actually entail that there have been infinitely many
chickens! (And also infinitely many eggs.) Thus, since there have only been
finitely many chickens in the history of the world, at least one of the premises
must be false. Premises 3, 4, and 5 are all obviously true (in fact, 3 and 4
basically state part of the meaning of "came before"). Thus, there must either
have been a chicken that didn't come from an egg, or an egg that didn't come
from a chicken (or both?). (Of course, this argument, and this proof, don't give
us any help in solving the ancient problem.)
